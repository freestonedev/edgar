Marketing reporting for the AI-search era

RoleSenior program manager, US marketing
Timeline2026 – ongoing
StackClaude, MCP integrations, Search Console, GA4, Gauge (AI visibility), Buffer
TL;DR

Search doesn't just mean Google anymore. I rebuilt weekly marketing reporting around that reality: AI workflows that pull Search Console, GA4, social, and AI answer-engine visibility (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews) into one weekly view, assembled by automation instead of a morning of tab-switching. It's SEO and GEO reporting in the same motion, and the GEO program it feeds has made the brand the most-cited in its category on tracked AI prompts.

Why it exists

Buyers now start with a question to an AI assistant, not a query to Google. When someone asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini a question your brand should be the answer to, either you show up or a competitor does; every answer you appear in is a lead you didn't pay for. You can't manage a channel you don't measure, so the first job was making AI visibility a number that shows up next to clicks and impressions every single week.

The problem

Weekly marketing reporting meant logging into half a dozen tools, exporting numbers into a spreadsheet, and hoping the definitions stayed consistent week to week. Meanwhile a growing share of our audience was finding answers through AI assistants, and none of that visibility showed up in the classic reports at all. We were spending real hours measuring a shrinking slice of the funnel.

What I built

  1. Connected our data sources to Claude through MCP integrations: Search Console and GA4 reporting, AI visibility tracking through Gauge, and social metrics through Buffer plus native platform dashboards.
  2. Defined a fixed weekly reporting window and a source-per-row map, so every metric has one definition and one owner, and the numbers are comparable week over week.
  3. Built the workflow that fills the weekly reporting sheet automatically: each Monday the numbers land, sourced and dated, and the humans spend their time on the "so what," not the copy-paste.
  4. Added GEO to the picture: brand visibility and citation tracking across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews, alongside classic rankings, so we can see AI-driven discovery, not just guess at it.

Why the GEO part matters

"Generative Engine Optimization" is where SEO was in 2003: the channel is real, the tooling is young, and almost nobody has operational experience with it. Tracking how a brand shows up in AI answers, which sources those engines cite, and what content actually earns citations is now part of my weekly rhythm. The same thinking drives how we build pages: structured data, clean crawlable content, and question-shaped copy that answer engines can lift.
