A website that publishes itself from a spreadsheet

RoleSenior program manager & builder
ForYC-Backed Company
TimelineMarch 2026 – ongoing
StackClaude, Google Sheets, Apps Script, GitHub Actions, static HTML
TL;DR

A tech leadership podcast, run by a Y Combinator-backed company, interviews the engineering and executive leaders shaping the AI era. I designed and built the publishing system behind it: an AI pipeline that takes a raw interview transcript and produces a live, schema-marked, SEO-ready guest page. Publishing a guest went from a multi-hour manual job to a reviewed, largely automated flow, and the system ships every week: 51 guest pages live from 74 transcripts since March 2026, 481 automated deploys in 4 months, and new interviews recording in San Francisco and Austin right now.

Why it exists

This isn't content for content's sake. It's a relationship engine. Every interview is a real conversation with the technical leaders deciding how companies build, hire, and lead in the AI era, and the series has widened from CTOs and engineering leaders to CEOs for exactly that reason. For the brand behind it, staying in that conversation is the strategy: the people on camera are the audience it most wants to reach.

That's why publishing velocity matters. An interview sitting unpublished in a folder builds no relationships and earns no search visibility. The series' second volume is in production now, with new interviews recording in San Francisco and Austin every week, and the pipeline keeps pace: at one stretch it shipped seven new guest pages in a single two-week span while recordings were still coming in. The system exists so the bottleneck is never production.

The problem

Every new interview created the same pile of manual work: write a bio, pull a headline and a notable quote, format a page, clean a messy auto-generated transcript, set metadata and structured data, update the index and sitemap, and get it all deployed. Done by hand, each guest cost hours and produced inconsistent pages. At one or two guests a week, the publishing work was becoming the bottleneck on the whole content program.

What I built

A pipeline where a Google Sheet is the CMS and AI does the production work, with a human approving at the checkpoints that matter:

  1. A raw transcript lands, and an AI skill generates the complete guest profile: bio, headline with editorial emphasis, notable quote, role, company, category, and URL slug.
  2. The fields are written into the Bios sheet through a bound Apps Script web app, so the sheet stays the single source of truth the team already knows how to edit.
  3. A human does the two things only a human should: approves the copy, uploads the headshot, and ticks the publish checkbox.
  4. A GitHub Actions build reads the live sheet and renders each guest's static page from a template, then regenerates the index page, summary copy, FAQ blocks, structured data, and sitemap.
  5. A second AI skill cleans the raw transcript (timestamps, chit-chat, filler out; real speaker names in) and bakes it into the page as crawlable HTML.

Because every page is static HTML with schema markup, FAQ blocks, and clean internal linking generated at build time, SEO best practices aren't a checklist someone has to remember. They're a property of the build.

Results

  • Publishing a guest dropped from a multi-hour manual job to minutes of human review on top of an automated flow.
  • Every page ships with consistent structured data, metadata, and a full cleaned transcript, which makes the archive crawlable by both search engines and AI answer engines.
  • Non-technical teammates publish through a spreadsheet. Nobody touches HTML to ship a guest.

What this demonstrates

  • Web production process design: intake, prioritization, human checkpoints, and automated execution, built so the process scales without adding headcount.
  • CMS architecture: a deliberately boring, durable choice (sheet as CMS, static output) matched to the team that has to operate it.
  • SEO and AI discoverability by default: schema, metadata, internal linking, and crawlable long-form content generated at build time, not maintained by hand.
  • AI workflows in production: not a demo. This system has been shipping real pages weekly since March 2026.

Process and architecture are described from my own work. Internal data, guest personal information, and business metrics are intentionally not shown.
